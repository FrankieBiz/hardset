# Hardset: business decisions and the evidence needed next

**Date:** 9 November 2026
**Status:** Planning recommendations. No prices, purchases, outreach, data collection, public claims or deployments are authorized by this document.
**Read with:** [Strategy and research](HARDSET_STRATEGIC_PLAN.md) and [Product blueprint](HARDSET_PRODUCT_BLUEPRINT.md).

## 1. Decide the kind of business before expanding the product

**Recommended default: a focused consumer software business serving self-directed lifters, with a useful free core and a paid on-device planning/review package.**

Do not design for an enterprise sales organization, a content marketplace or a subscription coaching service unless evidence causes a deliberate change. Each would bring different buyers, operations and product requirements. The first commercial question is whether a coherent personal tool can earn durable use and enough revenue to maintain itself.

The owner has endorsed the product direction, but the repository does not establish business targets, operating budget, team capacity or existing sales. Therefore this document chooses reversible defaults and identifies the commitments that require actual numbers.

### Recommended broader decisions

| Area | Choose now as a planning default | Reconsider when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business shape | Focused consumer app | A different buyer repeatedly demonstrates a stronger need |
| Buyer | The individual lifter | Coaches become repeat purchasers rather than occasional referrers |
| Main promise | Run your own plan across real setups; understand comparable progress | Observations show another outcome drives switching |
| Free product | A complete basic plan-to-review experience | Data shows a different boundary preserves value and supports viability |
| Paid product | Deeper block planning and review, offered as one coherent package | Retained users show a different willingness to pay |
| Billing direction | Prefer a one-time unlock for the defined on-device package initially | Continuing service costs or proven recurring value warrant another model |
| Acquisition | Demonstrate a specific real-world workflow; selective community and coach referrals | Retention and economics justify broader paid acquisition |
| Data | Local training data, optional sync, deliberate sharing | A specific valuable capability requires a separately designed service |
| Platform | Finish and validate iPhone first | Device exclusion becomes a measured adoption constraint |
| Operating pace | One major user outcome at a time | Team capacity and evidence support parallel product investment |

These choices are not validated market facts. They make the next experiment coherent.

## 2. Define an offer worth evaluating

The free product must be a usable training tool. Do not charge by logged set, workout count, machine count, amount of history or export frequency. Such limits would interfere with the continuity we want users to experience.

### Proposed packaging

| Free core | Proposed paid depth |
| --- | --- |
| Existing logging, history, gym/machine records and import/export promises | Multi-block planning and saved block-comparison workspaces |
| Basic user-authored routines and flexible sequence | Advanced plan versions and reusable variations beyond the basic version-safety required for everyone |
| Remembered equipment context and one-day substitutions | More detailed comparison filters and reusable review configurations |
| A straightforward last-session comparison with visible limitations | Longer-term review tools and structured coach-facing report composition |
| Access to all previously saved records and basic correction | The existing deep muscle map |

Basic correctness, accessibility, recovery, plan provenance and the ability to understand one's own records are never premium features. The paid package adds useful depth; it does not remove the free product's safeguards.

The plan is deliberately conservative about paid breadth. A customer should understand the purchase as “better tools to plan and review my training,” not a collection of unrelated locks. If the advanced package does not create enough value to pay for, adding more paywalls is not a solution.

### Billing recommendation

Start by evaluating a **one-time personal unlock** for a clearly defined on-device package. This fits the current Hardset purchase direction and avoids immediately turning a local utility into an ongoing service promise. It does not mean permanent maintenance has no cost.

This model is not unique: Liftin’ documents both subscription and one-time options; Hevy documents monthly/yearly and lifetime access. Their choices demonstrate available market models, not which one will work for Hardset. [Liftin’ press kit](https://liftinapp.co/press/), [Hevy pricing documentation](https://hevy.com/pricing)

Do not announce a lifetime entitlement to every possible future service. Equally, do not take away anything already purchased. Before changing the current product offer, verify what was actually sold, its wording and entitlements. Recommended default: include the expanded on-device package for any existing Hardset Lifetime purchasers rather than asking them to buy the same product again. This is a proposed policy, not an entitlement change made here.

A subscription remains a separate future option if a demonstrated recurring service supports it. Adding model calls or a backend to manufacture a reason for recurring billing would be the wrong order.

### Select candidate prices without pretending they are validated

Use **$39, $59 and $89 once** as illustrative US-dollar research anchors for the same defined package. They are hypotheses for comparing perceived value, not recommended live storefront prices, forecasts or commitments. Currency, taxes and purchase-channel presentation would be resolved before an actual offer.

Start with comprehension and tradeoff interviews. Later, test a real offer only when a usable product exists and a pricing test has been explicitly approved. Do not collect money for an imaginary capability or run a fake checkout presented as available.

Ask what the person would stop using or pay less for, and what would prevent purchase. Randomize offer presentation where feasible and record the order. Small samples can reveal objections but cannot optimize revenue or establish price elasticity.

## 3. Establish the business arithmetic that is currently missing

Before a paid launch, collect actual values for:

- Net receipts per purchase after channel deductions, refunds and applicable charges.
- Expected support effort per retained user and per imported history.
- Annual maintenance, device/testing, content and tooling costs.
- The owner's required compensation or financial objective.
- Qualified acquisition volume, observed conversion and realistic acquisition costs.
- Continuing obligations for existing purchasers.

For a one-time model, a simple planning check is:

**Required annual new buyers = annual funding requirement ÷ contribution per new buyer.**

Contribution must allow for support and continuing obligations, not just the purchase price. If contribution is zero or negative, additional buyers do not repair the model.

Illustration only: an annual requirement of $60,000 and $40 contribution per new buyer imply 1,500 new buyers each year. At $20 contribution, that becomes 3,000. These inputs are invented sensitivity cases, not estimates of Hardset's costs, net receipts, audience or future sales.

That calculation should drive a real decision. If the required flow of qualified buyers is implausible, narrow costs, change the offer, reconsider the commercial model or treat the project as a supported personal product. Do not quietly assume app-store discovery will supply the missing demand.

**Current block:** the owner’s business objective and real operating economics are unavailable. We can decide the package to test; we cannot responsibly declare a price or business model sustainable.

## 4. Choose a concrete acquisition story

### Lead with a situation a lifter recognizes

Working message: **“Keep your plan intact when the gym changes.”** Supporting promise: **“See what you did on this setup, and understand what compares.”**

Use this as a test against a broader alternative: **“Run your own program without rebuilding it every workout.”** If the broader preparation message produces stronger qualified interest, equipment continuity can become supporting proof rather than the entire headline.

Neither message should claim exclusivity. The earlier research establishes overlapping competitor capabilities, and a current hands-on comparison is still outstanding.

### The first demonstration

Show one lifter carrying the same routine across two gyms, selecting a different machine, returning to the usual setup and seeing separate performance histories. Show a rushed session that does not rewrite the routine. End with a review the lifter can explain.

A real workflow demonstration is more useful for this proposition than a wall of feature icons or a claim of superior science. Use clearly identified demonstration data until actual users consent to sharing theirs.

### Channel order

1. **Observed-use pilot:** recruit a small, qualified cohort and learn why they keep or abandon the product.
2. **Problem-specific educational material:** explain practical recordkeeping questions, such as comparing different machine loads or preserving a routine after a short session. Publish only after editorial review; avoid unsupported training prescriptions.
3. **Selective coach referrals:** let a coach hand off a plan and receive an understandable review without migrating their whole business.
4. **App-store presentation:** lead with the observed switching reason and demonstrable experience, then the ownership and privacy model.
5. **Paid acquisition:** consider only after users retain, a credible offer converts and the support economics are known.

These are future actions, not outreach or publishing performed during this planning task. Community participation should be transparent about affiliation and follow the community's rules. Do not mine personal identities or send unsolicited bulk messages.

**Decision:** do not build a referral-credit system or launch a creator marketplace before anyone has demonstrated an organic reason to recommend Hardset.

## 5. Protect focus while allowing useful distribution

### Coaches

Treat coaches as a possible referral and exchange channel first. Let a lifter import a coach's plan, retain authorship attribution and deliberately share a review. Avoid client billing, messaging, multi-user administration or prescription monitoring in the initial product.

A later coach offering needs evidence of recurring work that the individual product does not serve. A few requests to “send this report to my coach” do not establish a B2B business.

### Content

Curate for actual coverage gaps. Keep a narrow, reviewed exercise library and support clearly labeled user-defined movements. Track attribution coverage separately from whether an exercise can be logged.

Do not commission hundreds of videos or declare a universal muscle taxonomy complete simply to match a competitor's count. Every expansion creates a maintenance and credibility obligation.

### Devices and platforms

Keep iPhone as the proving ground. A future desktop planning surface could be valuable for long program authoring; an Apple Watch surface could reduce phone handling. Neither should precede evidence identifying that particular friction as the reason otherwise-qualified users fail to adopt.

Cross-platform sync, accounts and subscription portability would change the operating model. Those require a separate decision, not an assumption hidden inside a web-editor request.

## 6. Make privacy and support work together

Keep training contents on the existing local/private-sync path by default. Do not add a backend or a third-party analytics service just because a plan document calls for measurement.

For the pilot, use voluntary task observation and participant-authorized summaries. Ask permission separately for recording, screenshots and retaining a history export. Describe the purpose and retention period plainly. People should know they are participating in product research.

If product instrumentation later becomes necessary, specify the decision each event supports before collecting it. Prefer counts and state transitions over exercise names, loads, free-text notes, photos or full workout contents. Exact event design and privacy disclosures need their own review before implementation.

Suggested pilot measures:

| Measure | Operational definition | Important qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preparation effort | Time and interventions needed to get the person's real next session ready | Compare against their current method; separate setup learning from repeat use |
| Continuity success | Correct plan, setup and prior history after a gym change or substitution | Only include sessions where such a change actually happened |
| Next-session reuse | Person independently returns to the prepared plan for a later intended session | Do not equate daily opens with useful retention |
| Comparison comprehension | Person correctly explains the facts and exclusions in a review | A click or chart view is insufficient |
| Trust incidents | Missing, duplicated, misattributed or unexpectedly changed records | Investigate every report; a small sample cannot prove absence of defects |
| Support burden | Time and failure categories per onboarding/import | Research-team handholding can hide an unusable product |
| Paid value | Purchase and continued use of a clearly defined paid capability | Separate stated willingness from actual behavior |

Give users an inspectable support bundle before they share it. Redact by default and avoid secrets, full histories and unnecessary Health data. Collect enough to diagnose a specific problem, not everything the device knows.

## 7. Turn research into an executable next step

The [strategy](HARDSET_STRATEGIC_PLAN.md) defines proposed pilot gates. This section supplies the practical protocol. It does not claim interviews or usability tests have already occurred.

### Recruit for contrasting cases

Aim initially for 12–15 self-directed lifters. Include people who use multiple gyms, people who switch between machine variants within one gym, and a comparison group whose current single-setup workflow works well. Include some coach-written plans. Record which subgroup each observation belongs to rather than pooling all preferences.

Eligibility should be based on actual recent behavior and willingness to show the relevant workflow, not demographics or enthusiasm for a new app. Do not invent a detailed customer persona from a handful of public posts.

### Interview before presenting the idea

Use these prompts in roughly this order:

1. Show how you decided what to train in your last session.
2. Show where the plan came from and how you changed it most recently.
3. Tell me about the last time the equipment you expected was unavailable.
4. Show how you found the previous performance you used for that session.
5. What did you do after the last workout you could not finish as planned?
6. Show how you last decided that a movement was progressing—or not.
7. What works particularly well in your current app or method?
8. What would make moving your history to another tool unacceptable?
9. Have you paid for training software? What did the purchase replace or make easier?
10. If none of these problems were fixed, what would you do instead?

Follow the actual example rather than forcing every question. Record contradictions and satisfactory existing workflows as carefully as frustrations. Do not ask “Would you use an honest equipment-aware app?” and treat agreement as demand.

### Prototype task sequence

Use each person's real plan where practical, and separate demonstration records from private data.

| Task | Observe | Failure that changes the plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prepare the next session | Whether intention and actual history are understood | They copy a short workout thinking it is the full prescription |
| Miss a scheduled day | Whether they can resume the correct sequence | They must rebuild or manually repair the plan |
| Train at another gym | How much setup work is needed | Equipment administration costs more than their workaround |
| Substitute once | Whether today's choice changes future defaults unexpectedly | They cannot predict the effect of their change |
| Correct a logged set | Whether they understand what else changes | They believe an old correction changed a completed workout or accepted plan silently |
| Read a mixed-context review | What they conclude about performance | They infer measured growth or comparable loads across different machines |
| Export and leave | Whether ownership is believable and practical | Key context disappears or the user cannot access saved work |

Observe first, help second, and record every intervention. Counterbalance prototype versus current-tool order when comparing task performance. Do not interpret one faster attempt as a durable benefit.

### Evidence record

For each participant, record an anonymous identifier, behavioral segment, current tool, triggering event, observed task, workaround, consequence, intervention count, actual quotation if consented, contradictory evidence and the decision it informs. Keep personal identifiers and raw research materials outside the public repository.

A synthesis entry should contain: **observation, source, confidence, proposed implication, and what would disprove it**. Public anecdotes and first-party observation should remain distinguishable.

## 8. Know what would make us change direction

| Finding | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Equipment differences are rare and existing variants work well | Demote equipment from the lead promise; test plan continuity and review independently |
| Routines are useful but people do not use reviews | Do not force a review-led paid package; identify the repeated preparation value before expanding analytics |
| Users mainly want a program prescribed for them | Acknowledge audience mismatch; consider a separate coaching pivot rather than quietly changing the product |
| Users love reviews but will not switch loggers | Investigate a companion review/import product before rebuilding a full logger ecosystem |
| Coaches repeatedly assemble and exchange plans for clients | Test a narrow authoring/exchange tool before any marketplace or administration platform |
| Import/setup prevents otherwise-qualified adoption | Prioritize onboarding and data mapping over additional training features |
| A current competitor completes the same tasks equally well | Require a more specific advantage, pursue a narrower segment or stop the proposed expansion |
| Core value retains users but the paid package does not convert | Change packaging or commercial expectations; do not obstruct access to recorded training |
| Support or maintenance makes one-time pricing unsustainable | Revisit the business model with actual numbers, while honoring existing purchases |
| Native reliability is unresolved | Stop pilot expansion until the release evidence is adequate |

These are real alternatives, not a list of reasons to continue regardless of results.

## 9. Operating decisions that need work beyond features

### One source of current product truth

The repository contains historical claims about features, native verification and pricing that no longer consistently describe the code. Keep historical records, but separate them from a dated current-state document with evidence links. Distinguish implemented, tested on Linux, tested on Apple CI, tested on a physical device and actually released.

For now, these planning documents explicitly avoid claiming the earlier repeat-workout branch is production-ready. Before a pilot, inspect the current commit's release evidence rather than inheriting an old “green” statement.

### Release gate

A new capability is not pilot-ready simply because portable tests pass. Require the chosen build to complete native app/widget checks and the real user journey; verify recovery, offline use, imports and comparison attribution with representative records. Signed-device CloudKit and AlarmKit behavior remain separate checks. No unexplained data integrity failure is accepted as an ordinary beta rough edge.

Use staged exposure so a discovery cohort does not become an accidental production rollout. Maintain an export path before any migration or release that changes the data model.

### Capacity and sequence

Until capacity is known, plan one major outcome at a time. Reserve effort for diagnosis, migration support, accessibility and real-device verification. A feature is not small merely because its first screen is small.

Do not commit dates without an owner, available build/test environment and a bounded acceptance scenario. Identify which work can be performed on Linux and which requires Apple hardware or CI before scheduling it.

### Claims and learning

Keep a short decision register with status, rationale, evidence and reopening condition. Change a decision when new evidence warrants it; do not reopen it just because another tool or framework becomes available. Keep a separate research backlog so unanswered questions do not silently become product facts.

## 10. The point where further planning needs external evidence

| Unresolved question | Why more desk reasoning will not settle it | Exact next input |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Will people switch for this experience? | Competitor pages and anecdotes cannot establish behavior | Observed tasks with real plans and a short pilot |
| Is equipment continuity the main benefit? | Existing workarounds may be adequate or the segment too narrow | Contrasting multi-setup and single-setup participants |
| What can the business support? | No revenue target, cost base or support data is supplied | Owner's business objective and a measured cost model |
| What price and paid boundary work? | There is no observed demand for the proposed package | Comprehension research, then an approved real offer |
| Is the native foundation ready for a pilot? | Historical logs do not certify the candidate build | Current Apple CI plus signed physical-device evidence |
| How fast can the roadmap be delivered? | Team capacity and acceptance effort are unknown | Named ownership, availability and technical discovery |

**Planning endpoint:** the direction, initial product semantics, free/paid hypothesis, channel sequence, research protocol and pivot rules are now concrete. The highest-value next action is to run the observed-use research and establish release readiness—not add another feature pillar.

No participants have been contacted, no research data collected, no prices changed and no app behavior altered by this planning continuation.
