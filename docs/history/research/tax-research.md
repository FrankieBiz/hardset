# MUSCLE TAXONOMY RESEARCH (4 streams, UNVERIFIED)

## Hardset muscle taxonomy: a defensible rule for assigning primary and secondary muscles to movements, with per-attribution certainty

**Summary.** The decision is authorable. Pelland et al. 2025 (DOI 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w) does not just license a 0.5 credit — it publishes the exact operationalisation we should copy verbatim, and a table of worked examples. Their definition: for hypertrophy, "direct sets were those in which the measured muscle(s) was likely to be the primary force generator in the exercise. Indirect sets were those in which the measured muscle(s) was likely to be meaningfully trained but not the primary force generator of the exercise (i.e., synergist)." That is our primary/secondary boundary, word for word, and adopting their wording is what makes borrowing their weight coherent. But the paper says plainly that 0.5 "is still an assumption" and should be "a heuristic... rather than a definitive standard", and its corpus measured only eight sites: quadriceps, rectus femoris, hamstrings, pectoralis major, anterior deltoid, trapezius, triceps brachii, biceps brachii. Glutes, lats, calves, side/rear delts, abs, adductors and forearms appear nowhere. So 0.5 is a convention we adopt with a stated valid range, not a finding we inherit — and `EvidenceSource.validRange` (currently unused outside StrengthMath) is exactly where that range belongs.

EMG must be demoted to a veto. Vigotsky et al. 2022 (PMID 35006527) found no validation of acute sEMG amplitude as a predictor of hypertrophy and a weak mechanistic basis; Zabaleta-Korta et al. 2024 (DOI 10.3233/IES-230079) tested it directly and concluded sEMG cannot predict regional hypertrophy. Ban it as a source id, so an EMG-only attribution is unrepresentable.

Concretely: a closed 20-name camelCase vocabulary enforced by a Swift enum; a six-gate authoring rule whose decisive gate is "does this muscle work through a meaningful range under load", which kills the ten grip-only `forearms` secondaries currently in the seed; a hard cap of 2 primary and 3 secondary; certainty assigned per (movement, muscle, role) triple with `.high` reserved for uncontested agonists, Pelland Table 1 pairs, and trial-measured pairs; unattributed exercises rendered as a named remainder ("12.5 sets · 4 sets unassigned") rather than a silent zero. And record nothing about muscle length: the best-powered trial (n=297, 15 sites) found equivalence.

### Findings
- **Pelland's operationalisation of "indirect" is close enough to our "secondary" to borrow — but only if we adopt its wording verbatim** _(high)_
  - detail: The paper's Methods define it for hypertrophy as: "direct sets were those in which the measured muscle(s) was likely to be the primary force generator in the exercise. Indirect sets were those in which the measured muscle(s) was likely to be meaningfully trained but not the primary force generator of the exercise (i.e., synergist)." The worked example is 5 sets of biceps curls plus 5 sets of rows in a week counting as 10 total / 7.5 fractional / 5 direct. Three properties matter for us: (1) the label is per measured muscle, not per exercise, so a set can be direct for one muscle and indirect for another — which is exactly how a primaryMuscle/secondaryMuscles pair behaves; (2) the criterion is mechanical role, not EMG, not "feel"; (3) the authors state "This process was not wholly objective" and published Tables 1 and 2 so readers could audit the calls. The strategic consequence: if we define primary any differently (e.g. "the muscle the exercise is named for", or "the muscle with highest EMG"), the 0.5 weight was fitted under a different labelling and we are no longer entitled to it. Copy the sentence into the contributor guide as the definition of Gate 3.
  - evidence: Pelland JC, Remmert JF, Robinson ZP, Hinson SR, Zourdos MC. Sports Med 2025, DOI 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w, Methods §2.5 "Volume and Frequency Classifications (Total/Fractional/Direct)"; PMID 41343037
  - implication: Gate 3 of the authoring rule should be Pelland's sentence, quoted, with our name on neither side of it. This is the cheapest possible defensibility: the arithmetic and the labelling then come from the same source.
- **0.5 is a convention we adopt, not a finding we inherit — and the paper says so in its own words** _(high)_
  - detail: Three separate limits. (a) The authors: "it must be acknowledged that assigning indirect sets a weight of 0.5 is still an assumption. The appropriate weighting of indirect sets likely depends on several factors, including the hypertrophy/strength outcome measured, the specific exercise trained, the repetition range employed, and the training status of the participants", and it "should be regarded as a heuristic to improve the accuracy of dose-response modeling, rather than a definitive standard for practical application across all contexts." (b) The evidence margin is narrower than "very strong" for the case we care about: for weekly set volume and hypertrophy, fractional beat total by 2×Log(BF) = 9.48 (strong on the Kass–Raftery scale) and beat direct by 10.29 (very strong). So the win over naive total counting is real but is the weaker of the two comparisons. (c) The corpus. Table 1 lists exactly eight measured sites: quadriceps/knee extensors (incl. vasti), rectus femoris, hamstrings/posterior thigh, pectoralis major, anterior deltoid, trapezius, triceps brachii/elbow extensors, biceps brachii/elbow flexors. Grepping the full text for gluteus, latissimus, erector, abdominis, oblique, gastrocnemius and soleus returns nothing in the hypertrophy tables. Median fractional volume was 10.5 sets/week (mean 13.00 ± 8.87), and the marginal slope is quoted at the average of 12.25 fractional sets.
  - evidence: DOI 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w, Discussion §4.1 and Results §3.4 (2×Log(BF) 9.48 / 10.29); Table 1; Results §3.1 descriptives. validRange currently unused elsewhere: Sources/HardsetCore/Certainty.swift:51
  - implication: Ship one EvidenceSource for the weight whose validRange names the eight muscles and the ~0–25 set range. Any weekly total whose secondary contributions come from a muscle outside that list is capped at .low by Certainty.combine. This is the intended use of the validRange field, which is currently only exercised by StrengthMath.swift:24,:36.
- **Surface EMG amplitude should be unrepresentable as a source, not merely discouraged** _(high)_
  - detail: What sEMG amplitude is: the summed, volume-conducted electrical activity of motor units inside the detection volume of a skin electrode pair during a task, expressed against an arbitrary normalisation reference. What it is not good for: inferring muscle force or tension (the relation is non-linear and length-, velocity- and task-dependent); comparing across exercises, joint angles or muscle lengths (moment arm, electrode-to-fibre geometry and amplitude cancellation all change); comparing across muscles or people (normalisation is arbitrary); predicting hypertrophy — Vigotsky et al. 2022 concluded there is limited evidence validating sEMG amplitude as a predictor of longitudinal hypertrophic adaptation and that its mechanistic foundation is weak; and predicting where a muscle grows — Zabaleta-Korta et al. 2024 tested this head-on in 36 participants doing preacher and incline curls and concluded regional hypertrophy cannot be predicted by sEMG. A clean dissociation: eccentric actions show lower EMG amplitude than concentric at matched load, yet a 2024 meta-analysis found eccentric-vs-concentric hypertrophy similar overall. What EMG IS good for, and what we may use it for: establishing that a muscle is recruited at all in a task (a binary, which is what Gate 1 needs), onset/timing and coordination, and within-subject fixed-angle changes in neural drive. So EMG may confirm or veto Gate 1; it may never set Gate 3 or rank exercises. The trap is that EMG is cheap, acute, and produces one number per muscle per exercise — precisely the shape a catalog wants.
  - evidence: Vigotsky AD, Halperin I, Trajano GS, Vieira TM. Sports Med 2022;52(2):193-199, DOI 10.1007/s40279-021-01619-2, PMID 35006527. Zabaleta-Korta A et al. Isokinet Exerc Sci 2024;32(2):155-161, DOI 10.3233/IES-230079. Vigotsky AD et al. Front Physiol 2018;8:985, DOI 10.3389/fphys.2017.00985, PMID 29354060. Eccentric/concentric meta: PMID 39652733
  - implication: Do not ship an `emg` EvidenceSource id. Enforce it with a test asserting the set of source ids used by the catalog is a subset of {trial, pelland-table-1, anatomy, user}. An attribution that can only be justified by an amplitude study is then structurally impossible, not just against policy.
- **The decisive gate is not agonist-vs-synergist, it is "does this muscle work through a meaningful range under load" — and it invalidates 10 of the 50 seeded entries** _(high)_
  - detail: A muscle that holds a joint still is doing isometric work at a fraction of its capacity and is not credited at any level. This single gate removes: grip (`forearms`) as a secondary on pulls, shrugs and hanging leg raises; trunk bracing (`abs`) on squats and overhead presses; and spinal extensors (`lowerBack`) holding a neutral spine in a hinge or a row. The current seed has 12 `forearms` strings, of which 10 are grip-holds — pull-up, chin-up, lat-pulldown, five curl variants, shrug, hanging leg raise. Do the arithmetic that makes this load-bearing: a user doing 20 weekly sets across those movements is credited 10 fractional sets of forearms, which sits well above the 4-fractional-set minimum effective dose the same paper reports, without them ever performing a wrist curl. The app would then tell them their forearms are trained and suppress the coverage gap that actually exists. Supporting logic, stated as logic rather than as a finding: nowhere in Pelland's indirect column is a stabiliser — every entry is a synergist producing torque about a moving, loaded joint (presses→triceps, pulldowns/rows→biceps, pulldowns/rows→trapezius, presses→anterior deltoid).
  - evidence: Sources/HardsetCore/ExerciseCatalog.swift:239,:247,:255,:351,:359,:367,:375,:383,:423,:439 (grip-only forearms); :71 (abs on back squat); :303 (abs on overhead press); :271 (lowerBack on barbell row). MED of 4 fractional weekly sets: DOI 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w, Discussion §4.1
  - implication: Gate 2 is where most bogus attributions die, and it is the gate an author will be tempted to skip because "you feel it there". State it before the primary/secondary question, not after.
- **Ties should be broken toward secondary, because the two errors are not symmetric** _(medium)_
  - detail: Under fractional counting a set credits 1.0 to a primary and 0.5 to a secondary. Calling a secondary "primary" doubles the credited volume for that muscle; calling a primary "secondary" halves it. Both are errors, but they fail in opposite directions relative to what the user does next: an inflated count tells them a muscle is covered when it is not, and they remove work; a deflated count tells them a gap exists, and they add work. The first error is the one that costs training progress and the one the user cannot detect. So the written rule is: if you cannot satisfy all three primary sub-tests, choose secondary. Present this as a product policy with a stated rationale, never as a scientific finding — the literature says nothing about which direction to err.
  - evidence: Weighting scheme: DOI 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w §2.5. Asymmetry argument is ours, not the paper's.
  - implication: Put the asymmetry in the contributor guide as an explicit instruction with its reasoning, so authors do not silently re-litigate it 240 times. Where doubt is real and persistent, that is what .low certainty is for.
- **Outcome evidence should be allowed to override the prime-mover heuristic, with a mandatory citation — and the back squat is the live case** _(medium)_
  - detail: Plotkin et al. 2023 randomised 34 trained participants to 9 weeks of back squat or hip thrust at matched sets and found "Squat and hip thrust RT elicited similar gluteal hypertrophy, whereas quadriceps and adductors hypertrophy was superior with squat training" (gluteus maximus point estimates modestly favouring hip thrust but CIs spanning zero at lower, mid and upper regions). If a set of squats grows the glutes about as much as a set of the canonical direct glute exercise, crediting the squat 0.5 sets of glutes knowingly undercounts by half. Pelland's own caveat licenses the override: the appropriate weight "likely depends on... the specific exercise trained". So: back squat → primary [quads, glutes], secondary [adductors]. Kubo et al. 2019 (Eur J Appl Physiol, "Effects of squat training with different depths on lower limb muscle volumes") independently found adductor and gluteus maximus volume increases from squatting (+6.2% and +6.7% full depth vs +2.7% and +2.2% half depth), supporting adductors as a genuine secondary. The current seed instead reads primaryMuscle "quadriceps", secondaryMuscles [glutes, hamstrings, lowerBack, abs] — four of those five calls change: glutes is promoted, adductors is added, hamstrings drops to .low or out, lowerBack and abs fail Gate 2.
  - evidence: Plotkin D et al. Front Physiol 2023, DOI 10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170 (n=34, 9 wk). Kubo K et al. Eur J Appl Physiol 2019 (DOI not verified — cite by title). Current squat entry: Sources/HardsetCore/ExerciseCatalog.swift:70-71
  - implication: Allow exactly one override field per (movement, muscle) pair, and refuse to compile an override without a citation string. This gives authors a principled escape hatch instead of quietly bending the prime-mover test.
- **Cap at 2 primary + 3 secondary, 4 names total; stabilisers get no field at all** _(medium)_
  - detail: Two primaries only when the defining joint action is genuinely produced by two muscles that pass all three sub-tests — hip extension by glutes and hamstrings in an RDL is the canonical case, and the squat is the second via the outcome override. Never three. Secondaries capped at 3; if a fifth name survives Gates 1–3, drop the lowest-certainty one and write it into the entry's notes as prose. Arithmetic check that follows: worst-case credited fanout is 2 + (3 × 0.5) = 3.5 muscle-sets per performed set, and a realistic catalog mean should sit near 2.0. Assert both in a test. On stabilisers: no `stabilisers` array, no `isStabiliser` flag, no third role. Justification is that such a field can feed nothing — it cannot enter volume (no evidence for a weight), it cannot enter coverage (it would manufacture gaps that do not matter), and it would double the authoring surface across 240 entries. If we want to tell a user their core braces hard on a front squat, that is exercise-notes text. Note that per-muscle credits deliberately do not sum to 1: Pelland credits each measured muscle independently, so one squat set legitimately gives quads 1.0 and glutes 1.0.
  - evidence: Fanout bound is arithmetic on the 0.5/1.0 scheme in DOI 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w §2.5. Independent per-muscle crediting is implied by that section's per-measured-muscle classification.
  - implication: The cap is the mechanism that keeps a 240-entry catalog auditable by one person. It also bounds the worst case in the volume dashboard, which is the number the user acts on.
- **Propose a closed 20-name camelCase vocabulary, each slot defined by the joint action that loads it** _(medium)_
  - detail: Strings, as they should appear in the TEXT column: chest, frontDelts, sideDelts, rearDelts, rotatorCuff, traps, midBack, lats, lowerBack, biceps, triceps, forearms, abs, obliques, glutes, abductors, adductors, quads, hamstrings, calves — plus the existing sentinel "unknown". Defining each by joint action is what makes them authorable without re-reading anatomy: traps = scapular elevation; midBack = scapular retraction/depression (rhomboids, mid and lower trapezius); lats = shoulder extension/adduction; glutes = hip extension; abductors = hip abduction; adductors = hip adduction and assisted hip extension; abs = trunk flexion; obliques = trunk rotation and lateral flexion; biceps = elbow flexion (biceps brachii, brachialis, brachioradialis together); triceps = elbow extension; forearms = wrist and finger flexion/extension through range. Deliberate lumps: elbow flexors as one slot (Pelland's own site is "biceps brachii or elbow flexors"); no rectus femoris slot; no upper/lower chest split; no hipFlexors slot (leg raises attribute to abs at .moderate, with the execution dependence noted, because pelvis-tucked and hip-flexion executions differ and the right fix is two named entries). Use camelCase throughout and make the string the rawValue of a Swift enum whose case name equals it, so the compiler enforces the closed set. Prefer `quads` over the seed's current `quadriceps` — one dialect only. The ancestor app ran two dialects and MuscleTaxonomy.swift:170-177 documents the consequence: "lower_back" from the catalog and "lower back" from the lexicon split one muscle into two buckets.
  - evidence: Two-dialect bug documented at /Users/frankbisignano/dev/elos/apps/elos-mobile/Elos/Elos/Features/Train/Programs/Intelligence/MuscleTaxonomy.swift:170-177. Current strings: Sources/HardsetCore/ExerciseCatalog.swift:70 ("quadriceps") vs ancestor "quads" at MuscleTaxonomy.swift:48. Elbow-flexor lumping precedent: Pelland 2025 Table 1
  - implication: 20 slots, one dialect, compiler-enforced. Adding a value later is not a schema change, so the vocabulary can grow; what cannot be cheaply repeated is the human audit, which is the real reason to fix the definitions now.
- **Certainty attaches to the (movement, muscle, role) triple, not to the exercise — and only three things earn .high** _(medium)_
  - detail: .high requires one of: (a) the role is primary and the muscle is the uncontested agonist of the defining joint action — leg extension→quads, leg curl→hamstrings, curl→biceps, pushdown→triceps, lateral raise→sideDelts, calf raise→calves, bench press→chest, lat pulldown→lats, hip thrust→glutes; (b) the exact (movement, muscle) pair appears in Pelland 2025 Table 1, which is a published, adjudicated classification — this is what lets a handful of SECONDARY labels reach .high: bench press→triceps, bench press→frontDelts, incline/machine/shoulder press→triceps, lat pulldown→biceps, seated row→biceps, lat pulldown and seated row→midBack; (c) a controlled trial measured that muscle's growth from that movement. .moderate: anatomy uncontested, no trial and no table entry, or a close variant of one that has them — where most of a 240-entry catalog lands. .low: the label depends on execution, anthropometry or contested biomechanics (dip→chest vs triceps, squat→hamstrings, leg raise→abs, close-grip bench→triceps as primary). A .low name still enters the arithmetic but may never be the sole basis for a coaching prompt. .unevaluated: not yet audited — ship empty rather than guessed, and it contributes to no muscle's total. One consequence worth stating: bench press's existing entry (chest primary; frontDelts, triceps secondary) already matches Pelland Table 1 exactly, so this rule validates much of the seed rather than discarding it.
  - evidence: Pelland 2025 Table 1 indirect column (presses→triceps and anterior deltoid; pulldowns/rows→biceps and trapezius). Claim precondition: Sources/HardsetCore/Certainty.swift:96-110. Matching bench entry: Sources/HardsetCore/ExerciseCatalog.swift:174-175
  - implication: Because Claim forbids a value at .unevaluated (Certainty.swift:102-105), an unaudited entry cannot silently contribute a confident zero — which is precisely the failure the type was built to prevent.
- **An unattributed exercise must render as a named remainder, not a zero and not a poisoned dashboard** _(medium)_
  - detail: Certainty.combine takes the minimum, so one .unevaluated input makes a composite .unevaluated (Certainty.swift:33-35). Applied naively to weekly volume, a single user-created exercise would blank every muscle's number, which is worse than useless. The fix is to split the readout: `MuscleVolume { attributedFractionalSets: Double; unattributedSets: Int }`, rendered as "12.5 sets · 4 sets unassigned" with the unassigned count tappable. The attributed part is an honest floor at its own certainty; the remainder is named rather than absorbed. For the user-created exercise itself, the picker shows "Muscles not set" and a one-tap assign flow that walks the same gates in miniature: which joint moves under load → which of these muscles acts there → which one is the principal force generator. Name-based inference is banned outright; the app may offer a suggestion the user must confirm, recorded with source "user" at .moderate.
  - evidence: Sources/HardsetCore/Certainty.swift:31-35; UnevaluatedReadout at Sources/HardsetUI/CertaintyBadge.swift:92-108
  - implication: This is the design that lets the honesty layer survive contact with custom exercises. Without it, the first custom lift either lies (silent zero) or destroys the feature (blanket unevaluated).
- **Name-based inference must be banned by policy, and the ancestor's code is the evidence** _(high)_
  - detail: MuscleTaxonomy.fine(forMuscle:) is a 24-branch substring ladder whose comments are a changelog of shipped misclassifications: "lat" had to exclude "lateral" or lateral raises became lats; "rear shoulder" needed its own branch because 98 rear-delt machines fell through to a generic shoulder test and credited side delts; "hip flexor" had to precede a generic "hip" test or leg raises became glutes; generic "leg" silently returned quads; external/internal rotators returned nil, making 7 seeded exercises invisible to every scorer. Every one of those is a case where a plausible-looking string produced a confidently wrong muscle. The class of bug is not fixable by adding branches — each fix creates a new ordering dependency.
  - evidence: /Users/frankbisignano/dev/elos/apps/elos-mobile/Elos/Elos/Features/Train/Programs/Intelligence/MuscleTaxonomy.swift:130-155 and :199-208
  - implication: No string matching anywhere in the attribution path. Attribution is a compile-time literal for curated entries and an explicit user choice for custom ones. A suggestion UI is allowed; an inference is not.
- **Record nothing about the muscle length at which a movement trains a muscle — decisively no** _(high)_
  - detail: The best-powered test is a 15-site randomised cluster trial (Gschneidner, Carlson, Steele, Fisher, 2024; n=297 previously trained, 163 lengthened-partial vs 134 full ROM, 12 weeks) reporting statistical equivalence: arm muscle CSA -0.032 [95%CI -0.123, 0.058], thigh 0 [95%CI -0.094, 0.094], concluding between-condition effects are likely small and practically equivalent. Wolf et al. 2025 (PeerJ, n=30, pre-registered, powered for equivalence rather than difference) found no differences in elbow flexor or extensor hypertrophy. Positive findings exist and are real — Maeo et al. 2021 found greater hamstring growth from seated vs prone leg curl (+14.1% vs +9.3%, DOI 10.1249/MSS.0000000000002523), and Kubo 2019 found depth-dependent adductor and glute growth — but they are specific exercise-pair comparisons, not a per-muscle attribute that generalises to an arbitrary movement, and there is no validated scale on which an author could score "length" for entry 173 of 240. Where length does belong: inside the author's Gate 2/Gate 3 judgment. Pelland's own call that a back squat is INDIRECT for rectus femoris while DIRECT for the rest of the quadriceps is exactly a biarticular length argument, made in prose, stored as a role label. That is the whole of what we can support.
  - evidence: Gschneidner et al. 2024 multi-site cluster trial, sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/485. Wolf et al. 2025, PeerJ, PMID 39959841 (peerj.com/articles/18904). Maeo et al. 2021, DOI 10.1249/MSS.0000000000002523. Rectus femoris classification: Pelland 2025 Table 1
  - implication: No length or ROM column, no "trains at long length" badge, no volume multiplier. Saves a field, a UI affordance, a scoring dimension, and 240 judgment calls we cannot ground. If we ever want the insight, it is a movement-pair recommendation and needs no new column.
- **The permanence framing is stricter than the data requires: attribution values are rewritable, only the human audit is expensive** _(high)_
  - detail: CatalogSeeder runs on every launch and, for an existing curated row, updates catalogSlug, isCurated, modality, primaryMuscle and secondaryMusclesJSON while deliberately not touching name, notes or isArchived. So revising the vocabulary or a specific attribution in a later app version propagates to every device without a migration. And loggedSets carries no muscle column — sessionID, exerciseID, machineID, setOrdinal, weightKg, reps, rpe, isWarmup — so volume is derived at read time from current attribution, not denormalised at log time. What is genuinely expensive is the 260–340 hours of authoring, which must be redone for any entry a vocabulary change touches.
  - evidence: Sources/HardsetStore/CatalogSeeder.swift:61-71 (updates primaryMuscle/secondaryMusclesJSON) and :59-60 (preserves user fields); Sources/HardsetStore/Migrations.swift:203-215 (loggedSets columns)
  - implication: Right conclusion, better reason: fix the definitions now to avoid re-auditing, not because the bytes are frozen. It also means shipping a partially-audited catalog is safe — .unevaluated entries can be filled in over successive releases.
- **The same seeder behaviour is a latent data-loss bug the moment the UI lets a user edit a curated exercise's muscles** _(high)_
  - detail: CatalogSeeder.swift:67-68 unconditionally rewrites primaryMuscle and secondaryMusclesJSON for any row whose id matches a catalog entry. The comment two lines above shows the author already reasoned about this for name/notes/isArchived — "those are the user's, and overwriting them on every launch would silently undo their edits" — but muscles were not put in that set. If the attribution UI is exposed on curated rows (which the assign flow for custom exercises makes tempting to reuse), a user's correction survives until the next launch and then vanishes. Fix now, while the decision is cheap: either refuse muscle editing on curated rows and offer "duplicate as my own" instead, or add a nullable override column (additive columns are permitted; only renames and removals are forbidden) that the seeder respects.
  - evidence: Sources/HardsetStore/CatalogSeeder.swift:59-70; column-mutation rules documented at Sources/HardsetStore/Migrations.swift:15-18
  - implication: Decide this in the taxonomy spec, not later. It also matters for the certainty story: a user-overridden attribution has source "user", which the seeder must not silently replace with the shipped one.
- **Pelland's precedent table contradicts common app conventions in two places worth copying anyway** _(high)_
  - detail: First, anterior deltoid on the bench press is INDIRECT — the anterior deltoid row lists barbell shoulder press and barbell front raise as direct, and bench press, chest press and close-grip bench as indirect. Many catalogs make front delts a co-primary on pressing; the published classification does not. Second, trapezius has NO direct exercise in the entire corpus — its row reads "N/A" for direct and lists lat pulldown and seated row as indirect. Its dose-response contribution came entirely from indirect sets. Third, and usefully permissive: leg press, dumbbell lunge, hack squat, Smith machine squat and split squat variants are all DIRECT for quadriceps, so the squat-pattern family gets 1.0 credit rather than being demoted for being multi-joint. One mapping mismatch to record honestly: their single "trapezius" site does not map cleanly onto our traps/midBack split — pulldowns and rows load scapular retraction, so the precedent transfers to midBack, not traps.
  - evidence: Pelland 2025 Table 1, "Exercises counted as direct and indirect weekly set volume for hypertrophy" (DOI 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w)
  - implication: Cite Table 1 pairs directly in the guide as worked examples. It gives authors a published answer for roughly a dozen of the highest-traffic movements and settles arguments that would otherwise recur.
- **The dose-response above ~25 fractional sets/week is genuinely unresolved, and the paper's own wording is narrower than "inverted-U"** _(high)_
  - detail: Refining the brief slightly: the paper's best-fit hypertrophy model was a square-root form and it "supports the notion of diminishing returns but not an inverted-U"; the compatibility with other shapes is a consequence of credible-interval width — "given the width of the CrIs at higher volumes, the best-fit model is still compatible with multiple functional forms (e.g., functional plateau, inverted-U)." It also notes efficiency tiers indicated "no clear plateau" in the primary meta-regression, that the minimum effective dose occurs at 4 fractional weekly sets, and that "few studies have explored ~25+ fractional weekly sets." So the correct statement is: diminishing returns are supported; a plateau or inverted-U above ~25 is not ruled out; neither is monotonic increase.
  - evidence: DOI 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w, Discussion §4.1 (square-root best fit, CrI width, efficiency tiers, "few studies have explored ~25+ fractional weekly sets")
  - implication: Bears on the taxonomy indirectly but sharply: every attribution decision feeds a number whose interpretation above ~25 sets is unevaluated. The volume dashboard should stop asserting anything above that threshold rather than extrapolating the curve, and the validRange on the fractional-credit source should say so.
- **The vocabulary needs a permanent alias map, because user-created rows are never rewritten** _(medium)_
  - detail: The seeder only touches rows whose id matches a catalog entry, so any muscle string a user's custom exercise ever received persists forever in its original spelling. If the vocabulary is later split or renamed, those rows become unresolvable. The cheap permanent fix is a static `retiredMuscleAliases: [String: Muscle]` map from which entries are only ever added, never removed, consulted on read, with anything unmatched resolving to the existing "unknown" sentinel and rendering as unattributed. This is a five-line file that prevents a whole class of orphaned-data bug and costs nothing now.
  - evidence: Sources/HardsetStore/CatalogSeeder.swift:38-41 (matches by primary key only); Sources/HardsetStore/Schema.swift:22 (default "unknown")
  - implication: Ship the alias map with the first version of the vocabulary, empty. Adding it retroactively means auditing existing user data instead of just adding a key.
- **The authoring rule, ready to paste into the contributor guide** _(medium)_
  - detail: HARDSET MUSCLE ATTRIBUTION RULE (v1). Every entry names 1–2 primary and 0–3 secondary muscles from the 20-name vocabulary in Muscle.swift. Each name carries a certainty and a source. Work the gates in order; if a gate fails, stop — do not round up.

GATE 0 — Attribute the movement, not the machine. Describe the movement as a competent lifter performs it. Never copy a manufacturer's "muscles worked", a scraped body_part field, or another app; in the ancestor app 242 equipment records claimed "Full Body". If two common executions attribute differently (upright vs leaning dip; hip-flexion vs pelvis-tucked leg raise), ship two named entries rather than averaging them.

GATE 1 — Which joints move under load? Write down every joint whose angle changes against the resistance, and the direction. A muscle is a candidate only if its documented action at one of those joints opposes the resistance. Muscles that hold a joint still, grip the implement, or brace the trunk are not candidates. Nothing is attributed because "you feel it there".

GATE 2 — Does the candidate work through a meaningful range under load? It must lengthen and shorten under load across a substantial part of its available range. Isometric work fails: grip on rows, shrugs and leg raises (forearms); trunk bracing on squats and presses (abs); spinal extensors holding neutral in a hinge (lowerBack). Most bad attributions die here.

GATE 3 — Primary or secondary? Primary: this muscle is the principal force generator for the movement's defining joint action. Secondary: meaningfully trained but not the principal force generator — a synergist. (Wording adopted verbatim from Pelland et al. 2025, whose 0.5 weight we use.) Primary requires all three sub-tests: (a) AGONIST — the muscle's action IS the defining joint action, not an assist at a second joint; (b) LIMITER — at genuine task failure, this muscle is a plausible reason the set ended; (c) PROPORTIONALITY — selectively weaken it and the load you can lift falls roughly in proportion. If two muscles pass all three, both are primary (RDL: hamstrings and glutes). Never three. IN DOUBT, CHOOSE SECONDARY: a wrong "primary" doubles credited volume, a wrong "secondary" halves it, and the inflated number is the one the user cannot detect.

GATE 3-OVERRIDE — Outcome evidence beats the heuristic, with a citation. If a controlled trial measured that muscle's growth from that movement and found it comparable to a recognised direct exercise for that muscle, label it primary regardless of Gate 3, and record the citation in the entry. An override without a citation does not compile. Sanctioned today: back squat → glutes (Plotkin et al. 2023, DOI 10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170).

GATE 4 — Cap: ≤2 primary, ≤3 secondary, ≤4 names. If a fifth survives, drop the lowest-certainty one and write it into the entry's notes as prose. Stabilisers are never named at any level; there is no stabiliser field.

GATE 5 — Certainty and source, per name. .high = uncontested agonist as primary, OR the exact pair appears in Pelland 2025 Table 1, OR a trial measured that muscle's growth from that movement. .moderate = anatomy uncontested, no trial, no table entry. .low = the label depends on execution, anthropometry, or contested biomechanics; allowed, but a .low name may never be the sole basis for a coaching prompt. .unevaluated = not yet audited; ship it empty rather than guessed. NEVER record EMG as a source — there is no EMG source id, and an attribution justified only by an amplitude study is not made.

GATE 6 — Read it back before committing. "One set of X trains {primary} about as much as a set of {the direct exercise for that muscle}, and {secondary} about half as much." If that sentence is false, the entry is wrong.
  - evidence: Synthesised from DOI 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w §2.5 and Table 1; DOI 10.1007/s40279-021-01619-2; DOI 10.3233/IES-230079; DOI 10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170; and Sources/HardsetCore/Certainty.swift:88-126
  - implication: Six gates, one page, no literature lookup per entry. The two gates that do the real work are 2 (range under load) and 3 (Pelland's sentence plus the three sub-tests); the rest is guardrail.
- **Eight validation tests turn the rule into something a compiler and CI can enforce** _(high)_
  - detail: (1) Every string in the catalog is a Muscle rawValue — no free text reaches the column. (2) Primary count is 1 or 2, secondary count ≤3, total ≤4. (3) No muscle appears as both primary and secondary in one entry. (4) Credited fanout per entry ≤3.5, catalog mean ≤2.0. (5) Every vocabulary member is primary for at least one movement — a slot with no primary is a dashboard row that reads zero forever and should be deleted before ship (this is the test that decides whether rotatorCuff and abductors survive v1). (6) The set of source ids used is a subset of {trial, pelland-table-1, anatomy, user} — no EMG, structurally. (7) Every attribution at .high names a source of type trial or pelland-table-1, or is on the explicit uncontested-agonist allowlist. (8) Every entry is either fully attributed or fully .unevaluated — no half-audited rows. Tests 5 and 6 are the ones that catch the failure modes this research is about.
  - evidence: Existing precedent for schema-level assertion: Sources/HardsetStore/SchemaRules.swift:57-84; existing catalog test hook referenced at Sources/HardsetStore/CatalogSeeder.swift:34
  - implication: Cheap to write, and they make the authoring rule falsifiable rather than aspirational — which is the difference between this taxonomy and the ancestor's.

### Risks
- Users will read fractional set counts as measurements. "12.5 sets" looks like arithmetic on observed data, but half of it may rest on a 0.5 weight the source paper calls an assumption, applied to muscles the source never measured. If the certainty badge is not attached to the volume number itself, the taxonomy becomes exactly the kind of confident-looking fallback Certainty.swift was written to prevent.
- Allowing two primaries invites drift toward two primaries everywhere, since every author's favourite compound feels like it has two. Without the three-sub-test discipline and a reviewer, the catalog inflates every large-muscle total by up to 100%.
- The .low tier can become a dumping ground. If a third of the catalog is .low, the UI cannot meaningfully hedge and the coaching layer has almost nothing it is allowed to act on. Cap the proportion of .low attributions and treat exceeding it as a signal that the vocabulary is wrong, not that the movements are hard.
- Because loggedSets stores no muscle and CatalogSeeder rewrites curated attributions every launch, revising an attribution retroactively rewrites the user's training history. Someone who saw "18 sets of hamstrings" last month may see 12 after an update, with no explanation. This is an honesty problem even though it is not a data-loss problem.
- The 240-entry audit has no second reviewer in the plan. A one-person pass over 240 entries at 260-340 hours will drift in the second half, and the drift will be systematic (the author's mental model of Gate 3 will move) rather than random, so spot-checks will miss it.
- Splitting a vocabulary slot later (chest into clavicular/sternal, quads into rectus femoris/vasti) requires re-auditing every entry that names it. That is cheaper than the full catalog but it is the one cost that scales with delay, and the rectus femoris case already has published precedent pushing toward a split.
- Name-based inference will be proposed again as a convenience — "just prefill the muscles from the exercise name for custom lifts". It is the single highest-leverage way to reintroduce every bug documented in MuscleTaxonomy.swift, and it will look like a small helpful feature.
- App Review guideline 1.4.1 exposure grows with what the taxonomy asserts. A per-muscle weekly volume number presented as science-backed needs its methodology text to say that indirect credit is a convention and that muscles outside the source corpus are extrapolations. If the disclosure is generated from EvidenceSource records, that is automatic; if it is hand-written, it will drift.

### Open questions
- Should lowerBack ever be credited from hinges? Applying Gate 2 strictly gives no credit, since a competent hinge holds the spine near-isometrically. Whether the lumbar erectors hypertrophy meaningfully from deadlifts and RDLs could not be verified — nothing in the Pelland corpus measured erector spinae, and I found no controlled trial either way. This is the rule's most counterintuitive consequence and the most likely to be challenged by users.
- Does rectus femoris deserve its own slot? It is the one head-level split with a published precedent — Pelland treats it as a separate measured site, direct only for leg extension and indirect for squats, leg press and Smith machine squats. The counterargument is that no current or planned feature consumes it. Deferring means a targeted re-audit of quad entries later.
- Should the fractional weight be per-muscle or per-movement rather than a single global 0.5? The source paper explicitly says the appropriate weight likely depends on the outcome measured, the exercise, the rep range and training status. Varying it is more faithful to the paper and less defensible to state, since no per-muscle weights have been estimated.
- Does chest need an upper/lower split? Regional hypertrophy of the pectoralis major by incline is an active literature and users expect the distinction, but the Pelland corpus measured pectoralis major as one site and I did not evaluate the regional trials for this assignment.
- Should rotatorCuff and abductors survive v1? Both are defensible anatomically, but if no shipped movement makes them primary they are dashboard rows that read zero forever. The dead-slot test decides this mechanically once the catalog exists — the question is whether the 240-entry plan includes cable external rotation and hip abduction work at all.
- For a two-primary movement, should coverage and balance features treat 1.0 + 1.0 as legitimate independent credit? Pelland's per-measured-muscle classification implies yes and credits need not sum to one, but a squat crediting both quads and glutes a full set will read as double-counting to users and needs a product decision on how it is explained.

## Hardset muscle taxonomy: resolution decision — which muscles get their own entry, which subdivisions to reject, and the rules that decide

**Summary.** Recommendation: 23 muscle tokens in three tiers, not a flat list — 15 CORE (a zero is a real coverage gap and gates scoring), 6 ADVISORY (real muscle, real attribution, no defensible weekly landmark: shown as a count, never a warning), 2 JOINT-HEALTH (any/none, never a set count), plus the `unknown` sentinel the column already defaults to (Migrations.swift:102).

Two rules generate the whole list, and they are the actual deliverable.

RULE 1 — SPLIT ON DISSOCIATION, ANNOTATE MAGNITUDE. A subdivision earns a token only when a common exercise gives one sibling essentially nothing while giving the other a lot. Standing vs seated calf raise passes overwhelmingly: gastrocnemius +9.2/+12.4% standing vs +0.6/+1.7% seated, soleus growing in both (Kinoshita 2023, Front Physiol 14:1272106). Squats grow every lower-limb muscle except rectus femoris and hamstrings (Kubo 2019, 10.1007/s00421-019-04181-y), and 40° vs 90° hip flexion in the leg extension gives BF>100 for rectus femoris with strong evidence of no difference for vastus lateralis (10.1080/02640414.2024.2444713). Squats and hip thrusts both give gluteus medius/minimus "little to no growth" (Plotkin 2023, PMC10593473). Those are dissociations. Overhead vs neutral triceps extension is not: the long head still grew 19.6% in the neutral condition vs 28.5% overhead (Maeo 2023, 10.1080/17461391.2022.2100279) — a 1.5x magnitude difference, which belongs on the exercise as a lengthened-position attribute, not in the muscle vocabulary. Same verdict for hamstring heads and for the incline-chest claim.

RULE 2 — THE NAME TEST. An entry is admissible only if the exercise name plus one yes/no biomechanical question lands the curator and the user on the same answer. Splits whose boundary is a continuous parameter (bench angle, hip angle, ROM) or a within-head region (upper/lower chest, upper/lower abs, upper/lower glute) fail it and just relocate error. Splits along a joint action in a different plane, or a biarticular/monoarticular pair flipped by a joint angle already in the exercise name, pass it.

The most urgent action is not the enum: `secondaryMusclesJSON` is TEXT holding JSON, so a role field can still be added inside the frozen column — and it must be, because the seed currently credits abs and forearms 0.5 fractional sets for isometric bracing and grip.

- **The answer to 'what can a user actually attribute' is a testable rule, not a number: split on joint action or on a joint angle already named in the exercise; never on a continuous parameter or a within-head region.** _(high)_
  - detail: Every subdivision I accept satisfies one of two forms. (a) Different joint action in a different plane: front/side/rear delts, abduction vs extension, rotation vs flexion, elevation vs retraction. (b) A biarticular/monoarticular pair whose loading is flipped by a joint angle in the exercise's own name: standing vs seated calf raise (gastrocnemius/soleus), hip-flexed vs hip-neutral knee extension (rectus femoris/vasti). Every subdivision I reject fails on one of two forms: the boundary is a continuous parameter the user must estimate (bench angle to upper/lower chest; hip angle to hamstring heads; ROM to regional anything), or it is a region inside one muscle head that no common exercise dissociates (upper/lower glute, upper/lower abs, biceps heads, triceps heads).

  - implication: This rule is the spec's decision procedure and should be written into the taxonomy file as a doc comment, so the next person proposing `serratusAnterior` or `upperChest` has a test to fail rather than an argument to have. - **Pelland's fractional-counting result is confirmed as published, and its scope should be stated precisely in the spec.** _(high)_
  - detail: Pelland JC et al., 'The Resistance Training Dose Response: Meta-Regressions Exploring the Effects of Weekly Volume and Frequency on Muscle Hypertrophy and Strength Gains', Sports Medicine, DOI 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w, PMID 41343037.   - evidence: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/ ; DOI 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w

### Risks
- The gastrocnemius/soleus and rectus-femoris splits each rest on ONE small trial (n=14 and n=22, both largely untrained, both within-subject). Within-subject designs control inter-individual variance well, which is why I rate them high, but a single failed replication would remove the strongest justification for two of the four accepted subdivisions. Mitigation: both are children of a parent that keeps the published landmark, so a refutation costs a readout, not the arithmetic.
- The `traps` vs `upperBack` split is the one place I differ from Elos on judgement rather than evidence - I could not find any hypertrophy outcome trial dissociating upper-trap from mid-trap/rhomboid growth. If lifters cannot reliably decide whether a high pull or a face pull credits `traps` or `upperBack`, this split relocates error rather than removing it, and it is the accepted token most likely to be wrong.
- Splitting a parent token while keeping the parent's landmark means the UI shows two child counts under one parent target. That is honest but easy to render confusingly, and a careless implementation will invite exactly the fabricated child target Certainty.swift exists to prevent.
- 23 tokens means every custom exercise a user creates needs an assignment from a 23-item picker. If that flow is bad, users will pick `unknown` or the first plausible option, and their own data will be the least accurate in the app - worse than a coarser taxonomy they assign correctly.
- The stabilizer role at weight 0.0 will make forearm and ab volume numbers DROP versus any competitor using flat 0.5 counting. That is more correct and will read as a bug to users comparing apps. It needs explicit in-app explanation or it becomes a support burden.
- Pelland is a population-level meta-regression licensing exactly two weights. The stabilizer tier at 0.0 is an extrapolation beyond it - defensible as 'excluded' rather than 'weighted', but it must be described that way, not as something Pelland established.
- The Pelland plateau/inverted-U caveat above ~25 fractional sets/week interacts with token count: the more tokens share credit from one compound lift, the more fractional volume a program accumulates. Landmark bands calibrated at 16-token resolution (TrainingScience.swift:141-162) are not automatically valid at 23, and porting them unchanged would be an unexamined assumption.
- `neck` and `rotatorCuff` in a JOINT-HEALTH tier that shows presence rather than sets is a UI pattern that does not exist yet. If it degrades into a count with a hidden target, it becomes precisely the invented precision this taxonomy is designed to avoid.
- Freezing spellings now is right, but any token I have wrong is expensive to fix. The specific risk cases are `hipAbductors` (vs `gluteMed`), `traps` (vs `upperTraps`), and `abs` (vs `rectusAbdominis`) - all three are lifter-vernacular choices that constrain future anatomical precision.

### Open questions
- Is there any hypertrophy outcome trial - not EMG - dissociating latissimus dorsi growth from rhomboid/mid-trapezius growth by exercise selection? I could not find one; the lats/upperBack split, the most-used back distinction in the taxonomy, currently rests on moment arms plus EMG plus coaching consensus. NCT07360236 (lat pulldown vs row, latissimus and forearm flexor thickness) appears registered and unreported.
- Is there a three-arm trial randomising pressing vs lateral-plane vs posterior-plane shoulder work with all three deltoid heads measured? Every head-specific deltoid trial I found is single-head, so the strongest split in the taxonomy is justified structurally rather than empirically.
- Has the Chaves 2020 incline/flat regional pec result been replicated in trained lifters at a realistic frequency, or by MRI volume rather than ultrasound thickness? NCT07539337 (chest press variations and pectoralis major mechanics) exists but details were not retrievable. A clean replication would reopen the upperChest decision - the rejection most likely to be revisited.
- Does adductor magnus have any defensible weekly-set landmark, or is ADVISORY-with-no-target permanently the honest state? Kubo 2019 gives a growth signal from squat training but no dose-response; if a landmark exists it would promote `adductors` to CORE.
- How should the app credit the rectus femoris contribution of a SEATED leg extension - the hip-flexed condition Larsen et al. found inferior but not zero? A binary direct/indirect role cannot express 'partial for this muscle only', and the answer determines whether ~5 catalog entries are direct, indirect, or attribute-modified.
- Does SQLiteData's CloudKit path tolerate a JSON shape change inside an existing TEXT column across app versions while older clients are still syncing - will an old build reading the new role-bearing JSON fail closed or corrupt data? This gates whether the role field can safely ship after v1 or must land before first release.

## Hardset muscle taxonomy: permanent encoding, forward compatibility under CloudKit sync, rollups, naming, enforcement, and migration of the 50 seeded entries

**Summary.** The good news first: nothing needs to be renamed. All 16 free-text muscle strings in the seeded catalogue (chest, quadriceps, lats, biceps, triceps, hamstrings, upperBack, sideDelts, rearDelts, frontDelts, forearms, calves, abs, traps, lowerBack, glutes) are already valid camelCase keys. Freeze exactly those spellings, add three (rotatorCuff, obliques, adductors) for 19 total, and the migration is the identity map with zero orphans. camelCase is not a choice here; it is forced by 50 rows already on disk and it matches the schema's column convention.

The real work is three structural decisions, all of which must ship in v1.

(1) The unknown case. Do not put an `unknown` case in the muscle enum, and do not give the enum Codable at all — synthesized Decodable for a raw-value enum throws DecodingError.dataCorrupted on an unfamiliar string, which is how you get a crash from a peer's data. Instead: a total, non-throwing `MuscleKey { case known(Muscle); case unrecognized(String) }` at the storage boundary. An unrecognized key is stored byte-identically forever, counted into its own bucket so set totals still reconcile, excluded from every band and every group, displayed as "Not recognised" (never as the raw key), and — critically — it turns the affected period's coverage/balance verdict into Claim.unevaluated, because a coverage claim computed over an incomplete vocabulary is a false claim. Certainty.combine already does exactly this (Certainty.swift:33-35).

(2) No summing at the coarse level, ever. Two levels only (19 muscles → 6 groups), groups derived in code and never stored. Under Pelland's fractional counting one Barbell Row set credits upperBack 1.0 + lats 0.5 + rearDelts 0.5 + lowerBack 0.5, so a "Back: sets" figure turns one set into 2.5 — and there is no published band for a group to compare it against. The group type must have no set-count member at all.

(3) Two latent bugs in existing code. CatalogSeeder rewrites primaryMuscle/secondaryMusclesJSON on every launch (CatalogSeeder.swift:61-70); under field-wise last-edit-wins sync a v1 and a v2 device will overwrite each other's attribution forever. And `?? []` at CatalogSeeder.swift:128 silently zeroes every indirect credit on any decode failure. Both must be fixed before the column carries meaning.

The "optional muscle" flag generalises not to a Bool but to the absence of a band: `Muscle.weeklyBand(...) -> Claim<VolumeBand>` returning `.unevaluated`. That makes warning-without-a-band unrepresentable and keeps the flag structurally incapable of touching arithmetic — the exact bug Elos documented at TrainingScience.swift:108-114.

- **A synthesized Decodable on a raw-value enum throws on an unfamiliar string, so `Muscle` must not conform to Codable at all — the unknown case belongs in a separate, total boundary type.** _(high)_
  - detail: Swift's synthesized init(from:) for a RawRepresentable enum decodes the raw value then throws DecodingError.dataCorrupted when init?(rawValue:) returns nil; there is no built-in unknown-case fallback and adding an `unknown` case does not change that. Proposed shape: `public enum Muscle: String, CaseIterable, Sendable, Hashable { … }` with NO Codable, and `public enum MuscleKey: Hashable, Sendable { case known(Muscle); case unrecognized(String) }` carrying `init(stored raw: String)` (total, non-failable, non-throwing) and `var storedValue: String`. Because Muscle is not Decodable, no future code path can reintroduce the throwing decode; that is testable directly with `#expect(!(Muscle.self is any Decodable.Type))`.
  - evidence: https://sharojke.medium.com/handling-new-enum-cases-in-swift-unknowncasedecodable-protocol-08e54fa37aef ; https://nilcoalescing.com/blog/CodableConformanceForSwiftEnums/ ; https://github.com/swiftlang/swift-corelibs-foundation/issues/3690 ; existing precedent at /private/tmp/.../HardsetKit/Sources/HardsetCore/ExerciseCatalog.swift:483-484 (`modality: ExerciseModality?` — "a forward-compatible unknown rather than a crash or a silent default")

### Risks
- The tolerant decoder is itself a new silent-corruption surface: a decoder that accepts three shapes and salvages element-wise will happily accept garbage and report partial success. It is only safe because it returns unreadableEntryCount — if any caller drops that field, the decoder becomes strictly worse than the strict one it replaced, because it now hides failures instead of throwing them.
- Turning the coverage verdict .unevaluated whenever any unrecognized key is present could dark-screen the flagship feature for a user mid-upgrade — one v2-authored movement in a thirty-movement week blanks the balance readout. The behaviour is honest but the blast radius is disproportionate; it needs either a threshold or copy good enough that the user reads it as 'update the app' rather than 'the app broke'.
- The .stabilizer role at 0.0 is a departure from the method that actually won Pelland's model comparison, which has two categories (direct 1.0, indirect 0.5) and no zero bucket. A catalogue where authors default to stabilizer for anything uncertain is no longer running the fractional method at all, and the coverage bars would drift low in a way no test detects. Needs an authoring cap or a periodic audit of the stabilizer share.
- Committing nineteen keys now adopts content debt for at least three slots with zero exercises. If the 240-entry authoring pass slips, the app ships with declared muscles the user can see in a picker and can never train — a visible incompleteness that reads as a bug.
- Field-wise last-edit-wins means the no-rewrite rule in the seeder must be complete, not approximate. Any remaining write path that touches primaryMuscle or secondaryMusclesJSON incidentally — a rename flow, a merge, an import, a future 'repair' routine — will clobber a peer's or a user's attribution with no recovery, because the previous value is stored nowhere else.
- Adding a muscle in v2 permanently strands installs that never update: those devices see '≥ N sets' minimums and an unevaluated coverage verdict forever. There is no server-side backfill available, because the older build's parsing is the constraint. This argues for front-loading the vocabulary even at the cost of unauthored slots.
- camelCase is forced by existing data, but the likely content sources (Everkinetic, wger) use snake_case and lowercase-with-spaces. Every import needs a mapping layer — precisely the place where the ancestor's keyword ladder would regrow. That mapping must live in an offline authoring tool requiring human confirmation, never in the app.
- Six coarse groups with glutes folded into legs may test badly with users, who think of glutes as a category of its own (the ancestor made it a seventh group for that reason). Changing it later is free ONLY because groups are never stored — the moment any code path persists a group key (a CSV export header, a widget configuration, a notification payload), that freedom is silently gone.
- The proposed tracked/supporting split for traps and lowerBack means the app will never tell a lifter their erectors or upper traps are undertrained. Defensible as honesty about the evidence base, but also a visible feature gap against competitors that do warn, and it will read to some users as the taxonomy being incomplete rather than careful.

## Hardset muscle taxonomy: existing vocabularies, competitor breakdowns, and whether to align to any of them

**Summary.** Recommendation: define our own closed vocabulary of 22 lowercase snake_case keys in two tiers, align loosely to Hevy at the import boundary only, and record an Uberon mapping in code (never in the database). Do not adopt any external vocabulary as the stored representation.

Three verifications drive this. First, Uberon — the only permissively licensed candidate (CC BY 3.0, confirmed at obofoundry.org/ontology/uberon.html) — structurally cannot express the distinctions lifters pay for. `UBERON:0001476` is a single undifferentiated "deltoid" with no anterior/lateral/posterior head term; there is no clavicular- or sternal-head term under `UBERON:0002381 pectoralis major`; there is no upper/middle/lower trapezius term; and there is no usable group term for "hamstrings" (the nearest is `UBERON:0002463 muscle of posterior compartment of hindlimb stylopod`). A mapping would be lossy in both directions, so it is a footnote for auditability, not an interchange format. FMA is worse: name/marks restrictions plus its canonical licence page refused connection today (140.142.142.151:443).

Second, the market leader's vocabulary is a cautionary example, not a target. Hevy's own live spec enumerates exactly 20 values, of which `shoulders` is undifferentiated (no front/side/rear delts), `traps` and `upper_back` overlap, and `cardio`/`neck`/`full_body`/`other` are escape hatches that poison arithmetic. wger uses 15 Latin names with colloquial display names (4 blank). everkinetic's 23 strings contain literal typos — `forerm`, `bicpes`, `should` — shipped as vocabulary.

Third, the permanence pressure is narrower than assumed. `CatalogSeeder.swift:58-70` rewrites `primaryMuscle` and `secondaryMusclesJSON` for every curated row on every launch, so curated attribution is re-derivable forever. Only user-created rows carry permanent, CloudKit-synced muscle strings. The freeze deadline therefore applies to what the custom-exercise UI may write, not to the 240-exercise authoring effort.

Nobody found does fractional indirect counting. Hevy shows whole sets per group and makes no primary/secondary distinction in its charts. Pelland-aligned 0.5 counting is an unclaimed differentiator — and it is impossible on Hevy's vocabulary, because `shoulders` cannot carry a front-delt-indirect credit. That is the strongest argument for defining our own.

### Findings

  - detail: MuscleTaxonomy.swift:122-151 is a 24-branch ordered `contains` ladder over a 27-string vocabulary, whose comments record the bugs it caused: "Rear Shoulders" fell through to the generic shoulder test "so every rear-delt machine trained the wrong muscle"; hip flexors had to be special-cased before the generic "hip" test which "would otherwise swallow it"; `external_rotators` / `internal_rotators` previously returned nil so "7 seeded exercises were invisible to every scorer". It also contains `if m.contains("lat") && !m.contains("lateral")` — a lexical hack to stop "lateral raise" being classified as lats.
  - evidence: /Users/frankbisignano/dev/elos/apps/elos-mobile/Elos/Elos/Features/Train/Programs/Intelligence/MuscleTaxonomy.swift:122-151 (ladder), :128-131 (rear shoulders comment), :132 (lat/lateral hack), :145-146 (hip flexor ordering), :194-198 (rotators returning nil)
  - implication: Never ship a text-derived classifier as the source of truth. The mapping from any external string to a MuscleKey must be an explicit, exhaustively tested alias dictionary with no substring matching and no ordered fallthrough, and it must be allowed to return nothing.
- **Attribution must be a property of the movement, and the schema currently allows it to drift onto the machine** _(medium)_
  - detail: `machines` (Schema.swift:37-50) carries no muscle fields, which is correct, and `machineExercises` (:52-59) joins machines to exercises so attribution flows movement→machine. But `loggedSets.machineID` (:86-88) means a set knows its machine directly, so any future convenience that reads muscles off the machine row would be one join away and would reintroduce the Elos defect where 242 scraped equipment records claimed "Full Body".
  - evidence: /private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-frankbisignano-dev-elos-apps-elos-mobile-Elos/68d59098-d42a-4006-b2ce-c147b9defc03/scratchpad/hardset/Packages/HardsetKit/Sources/HardsetStore/Schema.swift:37-50, :52-59, :82-96; the 242 "Full Body" equipment records are an established finding in the brief
  - implication: Add a test that asserts no muscle-typed field exists on `machines`, and route every volume computation through `sessionExercises.exerciseID` → `exercises.primaryMuscle`. The invariant is cheap to state now and expensive to restore later.
- **Every proposed key has a defensible display name and a defensible exclusion — the merges are the decisions that need documenting, not the inclusions** _(medium)_
  - detail: Four deliberate merges, each with a stated reason: (1) calves stays one key rather than splitting gastrocnemius/soleus, because the volume landmarks in the literature are stated for calves and the seated/standing distinction can be added later without touching user data; (2) forearms stays one key rather than flexors/extensors, because no dose-response target exists for either; (3) hip flexors fold into abs, following Elos's corrected behaviour, because leg raises are anterior-core work and no one sets a hip-flexor volume target; (4) brachialis and brachioradialis fold into biceps as elbow flexors, captured as aliases so wger's `Brachialis` imports cleanly. `serratus anterior` and `tibialis anterior` are excluded entirely and alias to nothing — an import carrying them yields unevaluated rather than a wrong bucket.
  - evidence: /Users/frankbisignano/dev/elos/apps/elos-mobile/Elos/Elos/Features/Train/Programs/Intelligence/MuscleTaxonomy.swift:194-198 (hip_flexors reclassified from glutes to core as a documented bug fix); wger's Brachialis (id 13) and Serratus anterior (id 3) and Soleus (id 15) from https://wger.de/api/v2/muscle/; everkinetic's `ischiocrural muscles` and `deltoideus (clavicula)` from ek_ex.json
  - implication: Write these four merges and two exclusions into the freeze document with their reasons. A future contributor who does not know why calves is one key will split it, and the aliases are what make that split safe when it happens.

### Recommendations
- [hours] **Define our own vocabulary. Do not adopt Uberon, FMA, Hevy, wger or everkinetic as the stored representation. Ship exactly 22 keys, lowercase snake_case ASCII: chest_upper, chest_lower, lats, traps_upper, mid_back, spinal_erectors, front_delts, side_delts, rear_delts, rotator_cuff, biceps, triceps, forearms, quadriceps, hamstrings, calves, adductors, hip_abductors, glutes, abs, obliques, neck.** — Uberon cannot express delt heads, chest heads or trap regions — the three distinctions that justify the product — and has no group term for hamstrings. Hevy's enum collapses all three delts into `shoulders`, which would silently disable fractional indirect counting, the one differentiator no competitor has. everkinetic ships typos as vocabulary. wger's list omits rear delts, side delts, adductors, abductors, forearms and rotator cuff. None of the four can carry the arithmetic, so there is nothing to adopt.
- [hours] **Define a second, derived tier of 8 regions in code — chest, back, shoulders, arms, legs, glutes, core, neck — computed from the muscle key by a single switch, never stored. Map: chest ← chest_upper, chest_lower; back ← lats, traps_upper, mid_back, spinal_erectors; shoulders ← front_delts, side_delts, rear_delts, rotator_cuff; arms ← biceps, triceps, forearms; legs ← quadriceps, hamstrings, calves, adductors, hip_abductors; glutes ← glutes; core ← abs, obliques; neck ← neck.** — Two tiers give the coarse rollup users want on a summary screen without ever letting a coarse label become a stored value — which is how Hevy ended up with `full_body` and `other` in the same enum as `biceps`. Deriving the edge in one switch, as Elos does at MuscleTaxonomy.swift:74-84, is what stopped the two directions drifting there and is worth copying.
- [hours] **Forbid `back`, `shoulders`, `legs`, `arms`, `core`, `chest`, `full_body`, `other`, `cardio` and `unknown` as stored muscle values. Treat the existing `'unknown'` column default (Migrations.swift:102) as a sentinel meaning Certainty.unevaluated, and make every aggregate report the count of sets it excluded for that reason.** — An uncountable bucket in the same field as countable ones is what makes a set-per-muscle number a lie. Hevy's `full_body` value is the market's proof this happens by default. Since the column default cannot be changed, the discipline has to live in the type and in the aggregates.

### Risks
- Twenty-two keys nearly doubles Hevy's effective resolution and roughly doubles per-exercise attribution work versus a 16-key scheme. At 240 exercises the incremental judgement — chest_upper vs chest_lower, traps_upper vs mid_back, which of three delts — is real added authoring time on a workstream already budgeted at 260–340 hours, and it is the kind of cost that gets absorbed by lowering care per entry rather than by extending the schedule.
- Splitting chest into chest_upper and chest_lower is the weakest link in the proposal. Uberon has no clavicular- or sternal-head term, and while regional pec hypertrophy is a real literature, I did not verify a 2020–2026 primary source in this pass that supports attributing specific exercises to specific pec regions with the confidence a per-muscle set count implies. This is exactly the over-reading failure mode the earlier audit caught seven times.
- Hevy's `shoulders` value cannot be resolved on import, so every switching user's history arrives with a large unevaluated bucket. If the resolution prompt is annoying, users will pick arbitrarily and poison their own data; if it is skippable, the coverage screen shows gaps that are import artefacts rather than training gaps. Either way the first-run experience for the most valuable acquisition channel is degraded by our own resolution advantage.
- The Hevy enum was read from its live spec endpoint (api.hevyapp.com/docs/swagger-ui-init.js), which is an undocumented implementation detail with no stability commitment. An import map hard-coded against it will silently rot.
- MuscleWiki could not be verified at all — musclewiki.com/directory returned HTTP 403 and its API terms were unreadable in prior research. If it uses a materially different granularity that users have been trained on, this analysis missed it.
- Competitor granularity for Strong, MacroFactor, Setgraph and Alpha Progression is inferred from marketing copy and help articles, not from the running apps. None of the four publishes its muscle list. I already corrected one misattribution (the anatomy-map line belonging to StrengthLog, not Alpha Progression) extracted from App Store HTML that interleaves many apps' descriptions, so further errors of that kind are plausible.
- Commissioning a single body outline with 21 named regions front and back is a harder brief than a generic figure, and if the illustrator delivers merged or misaligned paths the taxonomy silently loses its visual layer. The art becomes a dependency of the analytics rather than a decoration of it.
- The `'unknown'` column default in Migrations.swift:102 cannot be removed and will keep accepting writes from any code path that constructs an Exercise without setting the field. Treating it as a sentinel is a convention, not an enforced invariant, and conventions in a permanently-additive schema erode.
- Keeping `neck` in the vocabulary but out of coverage scoring creates two lists that can drift. If a later contributor adds a key to one and not the other, the symptom is a silently uncounted muscle — the same class of bug as Elos's rotators returning nil and making seven exercises invisible to every scorer.
- Recording an Uberon mapping that is 'broader' or 'composite' for most keys risks conveying more rigour than exists. If it is ever surfaced to users as provenance, a knowledgeable reader will correctly note that three of our delt keys cite the same identifier, which reads as false precision rather than honesty unless the alignment quality is shown alongside.

### Open questions
- Is splitting chest into chest_upper and chest_lower supported by a 2020–2026 primary source strong enough to justify per-region set counting, or should v1 ship a single `chest` key and add the split later once the seeder-rewrite escape hatch is proven? The same question applies to traps_upper vs mid_back.
- Does MacroFactor Workouts weight indirect sets fractionally? Their team is the most science-literate competitor and their public docs are silent. If they already do 0.5 counting, the differentiator claim is wrong and the taxonomy argument that rests on it needs re-examining.
- What muscle vocabulary does MuscleWiki actually use? Cloudflare blocked every attempt in this pass and in prior research. It is worth one attempt from a browser session, purely to see whether a widely-used consumer reference has trained users to expect a granularity we are not offering.
- Should `calves` split into gastrocnemius and soleus in v1? Both wger and everkinetic split them, the seated/standing distinction is real, and drawability is marginal-but-possible — but no volume landmark in the literature is stated per head. Deferring is safe only if the alias table captures both terms now.
- Does the App Store's own metadata expose a machine-readable feature list that would let the competitor-granularity claims be verified rather than inferred, or is installing and screenshotting the five apps the only honest way to settle what each actually shows?
- Is there any real future scenario in which Hardset publishes or ingests a dataset where an Uberon mapping would be load-bearing? If the honest answer is no, the 22-line table is documentation rather than interchange and should be described as such in the freeze document.
