Hardset

The log that won’t lie to you.

A hypertrophy log for people who keep receipts. It runs on your iPhone, keeps every set there, and says out loud when a number is an estimate instead of a measurement.

The lead feature

Local-only is the feature.

No account. No server. No sync. Your workouts are stored in a database on your iPhone and nowhere else. There is no Hardset cloud to sign into, no feed to maintain, and no company holding your training history.

No account

Open it and log.

There is no sign-up, no email address, and no profile. Hardset does not know who you are, because it never asks and has nowhere to send the answer.

No server

Nothing to breach.

Your sets, machines, gyms, notes, and body mass stay in a local SQLite database. Hardset has no backend, no analytics, and no advertising SDK.

The trade, stated

The phone is the only copy.

Local-only means no automatic backup. Export a JSON or CSV file to keep a copy you can restore, and do not delete the app before you do. Deleting Hardset deletes the history on it.

Calibrated honesty

It tells you what it knows, and what it doesn’t.

Estimates

Never more certain than the method.

A formula-derived one-rep max is Moderate confidence up to 10 reps, Low at 11 and 12, and no estimate is produced above 12. Only a measured single is High.

Targets

The target line is missing on purpose.

No weekly set target is established for any muscle, so Hardset draws no target and calls the number “Not evaluated.” The research is cited in the app, including where the method is a stated convention rather than a finding.

Machines

Two machines. Two histories.

Training history is keyed to movement and machine. A 200 lb leg press on one sled is not the same lift on another, so Hardset starts a separate series and never draws progress across a switch.

Volume

Fractional sets, with the working shown.

A completed set credits 1.0 to each direct muscle and 0.5 to each indirect one. Tap a count to see the sets it was worked from. The 0.5 weight is an adopted convention, and the app says so.

Your data

Export is the backup. Import is the restore.

Hardset can write your history as CSV, JSON, or SQLite. JSON brings back everything Hardset can restore, including saved workouts and body mass. CSV brings back workouts. SQLite is the database itself, for keeping and inspecting; Hardset cannot read it back in.

  • CSV. Every finished workout, set by set, with the movements, gyms, and machines it used. A spreadsheet can open it, and Hardset can read it back.
  • JSON. The complete restorable copy: workouts, movements, gyms, machines, saved workouts, and body mass. A workout still open when you export is not restored.
  • SQLite. A full copy for inspecting or archiving. It is not a restore format.
  • Sharing is yours. Exports leave the app only through the share sheet you choose. The app or person you send a file to controls their copy.

Hardset does not sync between devices. An export is the copy that survives a lost phone, a reinstall, or a new device. Restoring a file leaves what is already on the phone alone.

150 movements

Researched, attributed, and yours to extend.

Every curated movement ships with its complete muscle attribution — direct, indirect, or stabilising — and the anatomy it was inferred from. Add a custom movement when the catalogue does not have yours; your attribution is yours, and Hardset does not overwrite it.

Attribution

Direct, indirect, or stabilising.

The role decides the arithmetic: a full set, half a set, or no credit. A movement’s primary muscle is always also labelled direct, so the label and the count cannot disagree.

Provenance

Every entry says where it came from.

New movements cite the anatomy and the loaded action they were inferred from. None claims a study tested a different machine or ranked a variation by EMG.

Extensible

The catalogue is a floor, not a cage.

Custom movements carry your own muscle roles, so a machine the catalogue has never seen still counts toward the right muscles.

Hardset Lifetime

One purchase. No subscription.

Logging, history, CSV import, and the CSV, JSON, and SQLite exports are free. The optional Lifetime unlock is one non-consumable In-App Purchase. It unlocks the deep muscle map for the week on screen. It does not unlock your own history, and it does not turn on a subscription.

Free, always

The log itself.

Recording sets, correcting them, reading history, importing a Strong or Hevy CSV, and exporting every format are available without a purchase.

$19.99 at launch

Muscle history.

A one-time purchase that opens muscle history: when each muscle was last trained, the last four weeks against the four before, your lifetime total, and the shape of your last twelve weeks. The standing price is $29.99 after the launch period.

Restore

Use the same Apple Account.

If you reinstall, choose Restore purchases in Settings. A temporary failure to load the product does not mean a previous purchase was lost.

What it is not

No feed. No cloud. No invented coaching.

  • Not a social network. No profiles, followers, likes, or leaderboards.
  • Not a cloud service. No account, no sync, and no server holding your history.
  • Not a coaching oracle. No auto-regulated routines and no claim to know your biological recovery rate.
  • Not a subscription. No recurring fee, no ads, and no selling your data.
